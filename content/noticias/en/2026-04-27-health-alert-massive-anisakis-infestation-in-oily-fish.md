The European Union's Rapid Alert System for Food and Feed (RASFF) has published a **potential risk** food alert over a «massive» presence of Anisakis larvae in mackerel from Spain. The notification was made by the Italian authorities, who detected the parasite during their inspections. Italy has halted distribution of the product from Spain and the affected batches have already been withdrawn from the market.

### Key points of the alert

- **Situation:** The Italian authorities found a «massive» presence of Anisakis in several batches of mackerel in supermarkets. It is the second incident in a few weeks: on 20 March, the parasite had already been reported in fresh mackerel from Spain.
- **Risk:** The parasite can cause digestive disorders (**anisakiasis**) and allergic reactions that can sometimes be severe.
- **Product:** Mackerel from Spain.

### Prevention measures

The recommendations of the Spanish Agency for Food Safety and Nutrition (AESAN) to prevent Anisakis are:

- **Prompt gutting:** Buy the fish cleaned (gutted) or remove the viscera as soon as possible.
- **Safety freezing:** If you are going to eat the fish raw, semi-cooked or pickled in vinegar, you must freeze it for **5 days** at **-20 °C** or below, in a freezer with at least three stars; if you do not have one, buy the fish already frozen.
- **Cooking:** Cook the whole piece at over **60 °C** for at least 1 minute. It is ready when the flesh comes away easily from the bone and looks matte.

### What to avoid

- Do not consider a thick piece cooked until all of it has reached that temperature; check by pricking it with a fork or knife.
- Avoid home-made preparations of anchovies in vinegar or sushi without first freezing the fish for 5 days.
