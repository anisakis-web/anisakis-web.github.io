A study carried out by researchers at the **Universidad Católica de la Santísima Concepción (UCSC)** has identified that the marine parasite *Anisakis*, common in commercially important fish, expresses **genes associated with allergens**, even after undergoing different culinary preparation methods.

The research analysed *Anisakis pegreffii* larvae found in species such as **hake, jack mackerel and snoek**, assessing their genetic response after common processes such as cooking, refrigeration and exposure to lemon juice. The results show that allergen expression persists in every case, which poses a **potential risk for allergic or sensitised people**.

The findings indicate that common practices such as eating raw or marinated fish, as well as domestic refrigeration, **do not completely eliminate the hazard associated with Anisakis**, and that even after cooking, proteins capable of triggering allergic reactions may persist.

**Implications for public health**

According to the research, none of the four treatments had a major effect on eliminating the parasites, and cooking was the most effective option only in some cases. Eating fish as **ceviche or poorly refrigerated** is therefore a significant risk, and whenever a fish is infected, swallowing a larva will be dangerous.
