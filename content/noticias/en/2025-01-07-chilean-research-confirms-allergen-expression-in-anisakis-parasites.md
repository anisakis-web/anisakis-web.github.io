A study carried out by researchers at the **Universidad Católica de la Santísima Concepción (UCSC)** has confirmed that the marine parasite *Anisakis*, common in commercially important fish, expresses **genes associated with allergens**, even after undergoing different culinary preparation methods.

The research analysed *Anisakis pegreffii* larvae found in species such as **hake, jack mackerel and sierra**, assessing their genetic response after common processes such as cooking, refrigeration and exposure to lemon juice. The results show that allergen expression persists in every case, which poses a **potential risk for allergic or sensitised people**.

The findings indicate that common practices such as eating raw or marinated fish, as well as domestic refrigeration, **do not completely eliminate the hazard associated with Anisakis**, and that even after cooking, proteins capable of triggering allergic reactions may persist.

**Implications for public health**

The study reinforces the need to tighten **sanitary control of fish** and to promote proper consumer information, particularly regarding adequate freezing and thorough cooking of fishery products.
