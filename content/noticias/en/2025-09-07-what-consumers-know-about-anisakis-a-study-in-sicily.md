A study published in 2025 in the scientific journal *Food and Humanity* has analysed **what consumers in Sicily know about anisakis** and how this parasite influences the way they buy and eat fish.

It is an **exploratory study** based on a survey of Sicilian consumers. The researchers asked them about their knowledge of the parasite, how they perceive the **food safety** and **nutritional quality** of fish, and their **willingness to pay more for anisakis-free products**.

**Why it matters**

Anisakis can be prevented with simple measures, such as freezing fish that is to be eaten raw or undercooked and cooking it thoroughly. But to apply them, consumers need to be aware of the risk. Studies such as this one help to identify what information the public lacks and to better target awareness campaigns and the labelling of fishery products.

**An issue that also concerns the sector**

Risk perception does not only affect health: it also influences purchasing decisions. That is why informing consumers properly, without causing alarm, is useful both for public health and for the fishing sector itself.
