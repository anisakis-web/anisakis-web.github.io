***AZTI data confirm the high prevalence of the parasite in the species most in demand among consumers.***

A report produced by [**AZTI**](https://www.azti.es/)(Centro Tecnológico de Investigación Marina y Alimentaria) puts the spotlight on the high presence of ***Anisakis*** in some of the most widely eaten fish species in our region. According to the latest available data, **100% of the European hake analysed carried the parasite *Anisakis***, while **in anchovies the prevalence is 20%.**

Anisakis is a nematode whose life cycle is linked to the marine environment. It develops in the digestive tract of large marine mammals, such as cetaceans and dolphins, and reaches fish through the food chain. Although the viscera are removed during processing, “**the larvae can migrate into the muscle”**, which poses a risk to consumers if the fish is eaten raw or insufficiently cooked.

- **Impact on public health:** The **Fundación ELIKA**, part of the Departamento de Alimentación, Desarrollo Rural y Pesca del Gobierno Vasco, estimates that anisakis is responsible for **around 300 allergic reactions a year**, especially in people aged between 40 and 60. Boquerones and anchovies are involved in most episodes.

- **[Trends in anchovy parasitisation:](https://dspace.aztidata.es/handle/24689/1837)** AZTI has been carrying out systematic surveys in the Bay of Biscay for more than a decade, analysing one-year-old anchovies caught in different areas. Although an exceptional peak was recorded in 2014, with values close to 90%, prevalence subsequently fell and has stabilised at around **20–22% in recent years**. In 2024, the parasite was detected in **61 of the 300 anchovies examined**.

- **[Hake, the most affected species:](https://dspace.aztidata.es/handle/24689/1548)** In the case of **European hake**, the results are particularly significant. Between 2019 and 2021, AZTI analysed 223 specimens from the north-east Atlantic and **anisakis larvae were found in the muscle of every one of them** (belly, loins and tail). The scientific literature confirms that hake is one of the most heavily parasitised species in Europe, with an incidence that increases with the age and size of the fish.

Other AZTI studies have detected anisakis in other commercial species, such as Sardine, Atlantic Mackerel, Horse Mackerel and Monkfish, confirming the **wide distribution of the parasite across more than 35 fish species**.

The experts insist on applying preventive measures:

- Gut the fish as soon as possible after capture to reduce the migration of the parasite into the muscle.
- **Freeze fish at –20 °C for at least 5 days** if it is to be eaten raw or semi-raw.
- Cook it to **temperatures above 60 °C throughout the fish (Including the inside)**.

– The data presented in this latest AZTI report reinforce the need to maintain vigilance and awareness both in the fishing sector and among consumers –
