**Anisakiasis** is common in Spain. A study published in *Scientific Reports* (Bao et al., 2017) estimated that **between 7,700 and 8,320 cases** requiring medical attention occur in the country every year from the consumption of home-prepared anchovies in vinegar alone. The OCU puts the figure at **around 8,000 cases a year**.

**Raw or undercooked fish**

The parasite poses no risk when fish is properly cooked or correctly frozen. The problem arises with preparations in which fish is eaten **raw, marinated or undercooked**, such as anchovies in vinegar or ceviche, if it has not been frozen beforehand.

**Viscera are best dealt with on land**

Another part of the problem lies at sea. When fish is gutted on board and **the viscera are thrown into the water**, the parasite continues its cycle. The Comunidad de Madrid expressly warns of this: throwing viscera into the water encourages the spread of the parasite. That is why it is preferable to process them on land.

**How to prevent it at home**

According to the recommendations of the Comunidad de Madrid:

- **Freeze** fish that is to be eaten raw or undercooked for **5 days** in a freezer rated three stars or more (so that it reaches -20 °C inside). If the freezer has fewer stars, it is better to buy the fish already frozen.
- **Cook** fish until it reaches **60 °C at the centre for at least one minute**.

Consumer information and good practices on board are therefore two key tools for reducing the presence of the parasite.
