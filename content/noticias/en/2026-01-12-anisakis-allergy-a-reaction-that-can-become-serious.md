Anisakis does not only cause digestive discomfort. In some people, contact with this parasite triggers an **allergic reaction**, which is one of its most worrying consequences.

**When it appears**

According to the OCU, the allergic reaction begins **within a few minutes or, at most, a few hours** of eating the fish. By contrast, digestive anisakiasis (when the live larva attaches to the stomach or intestine) can take up to one or two days to produce symptoms. Sometimes both conditions occur at the same time.

**Symptoms**

The manifestations range from mild to very severe:

- **Mild:** urticaria, itching and reddening of the skin.
- **Moderate:** swelling of the eyelids, lips or tongue (angioedema).
- **Severe:** **anaphylaxis**, with difficulty breathing, which can be life-threatening and requires urgent attention.

A detail that often causes confusion: many people who have had a reaction after eating fish tolerate it again later on, which indicates that the problem is not the fish but the parasite. In such cases it is advisable to see an allergist.

**What if the parasite is dead?**

Freezing or cooking fish kills the larvae and prevents digestive anisakiasis. Whether dead larvae can also cause the allergy is a question on which, according to the OCU, **the scientific community does not agree**. For this reason, people diagnosed with an anisakis allergy should follow their allergist's advice.
