The technology used at sea and in factories to keep anisakis off the plate was the subject of a report on the RTVE programme *Directo al grano*. This is the field in which the Vigo-based company [**Marexi**](https://marexi.com/) (Tecnología Marina Ximo, S.L.) works, making equipment for two different stages: treating viscera on the vessel and detecting the parasite in fish.

**TREATING VISCERA BEFORE THEY GO BACK TO THE SEA**

Anisakis larvae are concentrated mainly in the viscera. When fish are gutted on board and the waste is thrown into the sea, other animals can eat it and the parasite continues its cycle. According to the final report of the European **PARASITE** project, the Spanish fleet fishing in the Grand Sole area discards around **2,233 million viable larvae a year** at sea.

The **Tedepad** equipment is fitted into the vessel's viscera extraction pipeline and applies **microwave energy** to the viscera as they pass through, according to the company's description. It is the result of a collaboration between Marexi, which is responsible for design and distribution, and **Industrias Ferri**, which manufactures, installs and maintains it.

The TEDEPAD prototype was developed within PARASITE, a project under the EU's Seventh Framework Programme (2013-2016) coordinated by the **CSIC**, with partners from 13 countries. It was installed on the 35-metre trawler *Vispón*, with a researcher from the Instituto de Investigaciones Marinas (IIM-CSIC) on board during fishing trips in 2014 and 2015. The report states that **no viable larvae were found** in any of the treated samples.

The company adds that in 2024 the IIM-CSIC validated the equipment again within **GESTPAR**, a project of Spain's General Secretariat of Fisheries (Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food) which, according to the agreement published in the BOE (Spain's Official State Gazette), includes validating parasite-inactivation technologies in the laboratory and on board.

**DETECTING THE PARASITE IN FISH**

The second line, **Anisakischeck**, brings together detection systems for industry and laboratories:

- **Anisakischeck Inline:** an optical scanner with artificial intelligence for pieces moving along a conveyor belt, at up to 1.2 m/s.
- **ScanisakiS:** analyses individual pieces and shows the number, position and infection percentage.
- **ScanisakiS UVPress:** counts larvae in samples under ultraviolet light, following the method of the ISO/DIS 23036 standard; it is designed for laboratories and quality control.
