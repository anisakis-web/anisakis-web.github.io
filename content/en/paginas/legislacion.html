--- title: "Legislation" slug: "legislation" ---

During the 1990s, the European Union established, through an extensive body of food legislation, the principles that form the common basis for the hygienic production and marketing of all foodstuffs, that is, the rules and procedures concerning the responsibilities of manufacturers and of the competent authorities, the structural, operational and hygiene requirements for establishments, the procedures for the approval of establishments, the requirements for storage and transport, and health marking.

In an exercise of consolidation, updating and simplification, the Council and the European Parliament have adopted a set of Regulations and Directives that restructure and update the hygiene rules for all foodstuffs, including products of animal origin, and expressly repeal all the European legislation that previously laid them down.

The revised rules are structured within a common framework, namely Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2004 on the hygiene of foodstuffs, which sets out, in general terms, an integrated approach to ensuring food safety from the place of primary production through to placing on the market or export. For its part, Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2004 laying down specific hygiene rules for food of animal origin.

Thirdly, Regulation (EC) No 854/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2004 laying down specific rules for the organisation of official controls on products of animal origin intended for human consumption deals with the specific aspects associated with those products when applying Regulation (EC) No 882/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2004 on official controls performed to ensure the verification of compliance with feed and food law, animal health and animal welfare rules.

In view of the existence of this regulatory framework, it became necessary at Community level and, consequently, at Spanish level, to adopt a provision clarifying the regulatory framework.

Directive 2004/41/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 21 April 2004 repealing certain Directives concerning food hygiene and health conditions for the production and placing on the market of certain products of animal origin intended for human consumption and amending Council Directives 89/662/EEC and 92/118/EEC and Council Decision 95/408/EC achieved that aim, repealing the sixteen specific directives on products of animal origin, while Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 expressly repealed Council Directive 93/43/EEC of 14 June 1993 on the hygiene of foodstuffs. Within the Spanish legislative framework, this Directive is transposed by means of the following provisions:

  • Royal Decree 640/2006 of 26 May regulating certain conditions for the application of Community provisions on hygiene and on the production and marketing of foodstuffs.
  • Royal Decree 639/2006 of 26 May amending Royal Decree 2551/1994 of 29 December laying down the animal health and public health conditions applicable to trade in and imports of products not subject to the specific rules laid down in Royal Decree 49/1993 of 15 January and in Royal Decree 1316/1992 of 30 October.
  • Order PRE/1764/2006 of 5 June amending Annex A to Royal Decree 49/1993 of 15 January on veterinary checks applicable to intra-Community trade in products of origin.

Immediately after the adoption of the package of Community provisions, the Commission began work on implementing the aforementioned Regulations and adopted, by means of the comitology procedure, the following provisions, which are under constant review and amendment:

  • Commission Regulation (EC) No 2073/2005 of 15 November 2005 on microbiological criteria for foodstuffs
  • Commission Regulation (EC) No 2074/2005, of 5 December 2005 laying down implementing measures for certain products under Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council and for the organisation of official controls under Regulations (EC) No 854/2004 and (EC) No 882/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council, derogating from Regulation (EC) No 852/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council and amending Regulations (EC) No 853/2004 and (EC) No 854/2004
  • Commission Regulation (EC) No 1162/2009 of 30 November 2009 laying down transitional measures for the implementation of Regulations (EC) No 853/2004, (EC) No 854/2004 and (EC) No 882/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council.
  • Commission Regulation (EU) No 1276/2011, of 8 December 2011 amending Annex III to Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards the treatment to kill viable parasites in fishery products for human consumption.

For a full understanding of the various aspects of the aforementioned Regulations, it is essential to be familiar with other parts of the legislation and, in particular, with the principles and definitions laid down in:

  • Regulation (EC) No 178/2002 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 28 January 2002 laying down the general principles and requirements of food law, establishing the European Food Safety Authority and laying down procedures in matters of food safety
  • Regulation (EC) No 882/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2004 on official controls performed to ensure the verification of compliance with feed and food law, animal health and animal welfare rules.

Specifically regarding Anisakis:

Although Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 29 April 2004 on the hygiene of foodstuffs requires operators placing on the market raw or almost raw fishery products to subject those products to freezing at -20 °C for a period of at least 24 hours, in order to help solve the problem Royal Decree 1420/2006 of 1 December was published on the prevention of anisakis parasitosis in fishery products supplied by establishments serving food to final consumers or to mass caterers.

This legislative act supplements the Community rules by establishing the obligation for establishments to inform consumers that the fishery products covered by the Royal Decree have been subjected to freezing under the terms laid down. In addition, it establishes a series of actions to be carried out by AESAN, namely: supporting the sector concerned by designing or supplying information materials to facilitate compliance with these obligations, launching a general health control plan for anisakis parasitosis, establishing and disseminating the technical criteria needed to determine in which cases freezing is necessary, and specifying the information to be provided to consumers as well as the training of staff in the establishments concerned.
