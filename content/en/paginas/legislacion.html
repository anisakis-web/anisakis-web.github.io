SummaryKey points on anisakis

  • Fishery products eaten raw

    Regulation (EC) 853/2004 (Annex III, Section VIII, Chapter III, point D, as worded by Regulation (EU) 1276/2011) requires food business operators to freeze fish intended to be eaten raw or almost raw at −20 °C for at least 24 hours, or at −35 °C for at least 15 hours, in all parts of the product.

  • Restaurants, canteens and retail in Spain

    Article 8 of Royal Decree 1021/2022 requires establishments that serve food, or prepare these products for the final consumer, to freeze fish and cephalopods eaten raw, pickled, salted or otherwise insufficiently treated (−20 °C for 24 h or −35 °C for 15 h), and to inform consumers through notices, menus or other means. Freezing is not needed if the product is cooked to 60 °C or more at the core for at least 1 minute. It replaces the former Royal Decree 1420/2006.

  • At home

    The consumer recommendations of the Spanish Agency for Food Safety and Nutrition (AESAN) are on the Anisakis page.

01EU legislation

During the 1990s, the European Union laid down common principles for the hygienic production and marketing of food. In 2004 the Council and the European Parliament adopted the so-called “hygiene package”, a set of regulations and directives that restructured and updated these rules for all foodstuffs, including those of animal origin, and repealed the previous legislation. These rules have since been further developed and updated.

  • Regulation (EC) 178/2002In force, amended

    28 January 2002 · General principles and requirements of food law; establishes the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA).

  • Regulation (EC) 852/2004In force, amended

    29 April 2004 · Hygiene of foodstuffs: common framework from primary production to sale.

  • Regulation (EC) 853/2004In force, amended

    29 April 2004 · Specific hygiene rules for food of animal origin, including the freezing treatment against parasites in fishery products.

  • Regulation (EU) 1276/2011In force

    8 December 2011 · Amends Annex III to Regulation 853/2004 as regards the treatment to kill viable parasites in fishery products. Its content is now part of Regulation 853/2004.

  • Regulation (EU) 2017/625In force

    15 March 2017 · Official controls along the agri-food chain. Replaced Regulations 854/2004 and 882/2004.

  • Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/624In force

    8 February 2019 · Specific rules for official controls on products of animal origin, supplementing Regulation 2017/625.

  • Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/627In force

    15 March 2019 · Uniform practical arrangements for official controls on products of animal origin.

  • Regulation (EC) 2073/2005In force, amended

    15 November 2005 · Microbiological criteria for foodstuffs.

  • Regulation (EC) 2074/2005Partly in force

    5 December 2005 · Implementing measures for the hygiene regulations. Part of its content has been replaced by Regulation 2017/625.

  • Directive 2004/41/ECIn force

    21 April 2004 · Repeals the earlier directives on the hygiene of foodstuffs.

02Spanish legislation

  • Royal Decree 1021/2022In force

    13 December 2022 · Hygiene requirements for retail and for establishments serving food. Its Article 8 regulates the prevention of anisakis.

  • Royal Decree 639/2006Amending act

    26 May 2006 · Amends Royal Decree 2551/1994 on health conditions for trade and imports of products.

  • Order PRE/1764/2006Amending act

    5 June 2006 · Amends Annex A to Royal Decree 49/1993 on veterinary checks in intra-EU trade.

03Codex Alimentarius

  • Guidelines on the application of general principles of food hygiene to the control of foodborne parasites (CXG 88-2016)

    2016 · Adopted by the Codex Alimentarius Commission in 2016. They were prepared at the 47th session of the Codex Committee on Food Hygiene (report REP16/FH).

  • Code of practice for fish and fishery products (CXC 52-2003)

    2003 · Includes, among other measures, the freezing of fish intended to be eaten raw in order to kill parasites. Text (PDF, in Spanish)
