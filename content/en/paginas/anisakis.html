--- title: "Anisakis" slug: "anisakis" ---

Anisakiasis is a parasitic zoonosis caused by the accidental ingestion of fish contaminated with larvae of nematode worms of the family Anisakidae. The clinical picture it produces and the associated pathologies are of two types: digestive disorders and allergic reactions. In both cases, “gastro-allergic anisakiasis”, the consequences can be serious, associated with the accidental ingestion of the parasite and subsequent immunological sensitisation to its antigens, which can be allergenic even when the fish is eaten cooked or frozen (Alonso-Gómez A., et al. 2004, del Caballero ML and Moneo, I., 2004).

According to epidemiological data (Audicana et al., 2002), there has been a notable increase in the affected population, especially in countries and regions where a diet based on fishery products is deeply rooted and, above all, where these products are consumed raw or only lightly cooked.

The life cycle of anisakids takes place in the marine environment, requiring one or more intermediate and paratenic hosts for the development and dispersal of the larval stages, which reach maturity in many species of marine mammals (their definitive hosts). The parasite’s successful life strategy has its origin in an evolutionary strategy centred on adapting its life cycle to the trophic ecology of marine ecosystems (Pascual et al., 2007).

The bottleneck in achieving greater prevention and control of gastro-allergic anisakiasis is prophylaxis against the parasite. Good practice programmes linked to legislative measures (Royal Decree of 2006) have been introduced, clinical diagnostic methods have been improved and expanded, and work is under way in the industry to minimise the presence of the parasite in fishery products, especially in production lines for fish muscle intended for human consumption. Within this line of work there are currently two approaches: on the one hand, improving detection and diagnostic systems in the edible part of the fish; on the other, the set of technological measures aimed at inactivating the parasite in fishery products or minimising its presence in exploited stocks. It is here that on-board sanitisation and prevention methods can be important.
